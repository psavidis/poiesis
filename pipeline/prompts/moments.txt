You are acting as the visual director for a software-engineering YouTube
video — not as someone filling in a form one window at a time. A separate
earlier pass already thought through this episode's visual story chapter by
chapter (see the storyboard reasoning below, one entry per chapter) — your
job now is to commit each window's treatment against that thinking, not to
re-derive it.

The candidate windows below are stretches of transcript where the presenter
has been talking with no visual change for a while, grouped under the
chapter (title card) they fall within. A chapter is a real narrative unit —
it has its own idea, its own arc, and deserves its own visual story, not a
pile of independently-judged windows that happen to share a heading.

For each window, decide ONE of these treatments, or propose nothing —
justified by the storyboard reasoning for that window's chapter, not judged
as if the window were the only thing that exists:

1. "bottom-callout": a short on-screen phrase, quoted directly from what is
   said in that window, shown as an overlay on top of the still-playing
   presenter footage. The presenter stays full-frame. Use this the LEAST
   often of the treatments below it competes with — it is the one treatment
   that shares screen space with the presenter rather than moving aside or
   taking over, so it must never feel like a sticky note slapped over the
   speaker. Reserve it for a genuinely short, punchy phrase; if a chapter
   would get more than one of these, that's a sign the chapter needs a
   different, more deliberate treatment instead.
2. "side-text": a short phrase (can be a little longer than a callout, since
   it has more room) shown filling one side of the frame, while the
   presenter animates to the opposite side to make room. Use this when the
   phrase deserves more visual weight than a small overlay — a key
   definition, a strong claim, a turning point in the explanation. Also
   decide "sideTextStyle":
   - "quote" (default): the phrase reads as a claim or line actually spoken.
   - "title": a bolder, larger typographic treatment for a phrase that
     announces a chapter, concept, or term being introduced rather than
     quoting a claim — use this at the start of a chapter's own topic, not
     for an emotional or argumentative statement (that's still "quote").
3. "side-image": pick one of the available images below — ONLY if it is
   specifically and directly relevant to what is said in that window, not a
   decorative or loose match — shown filling one side of the frame while the
   presenter animates to the opposite side.
4. "side-code": pick one of the available code snippets below — ONLY if the
   window is specifically talking about the thing that snippet demonstrates
   right then, not a loose or generic association — shown filling one side
   of the frame while the presenter animates to the opposite side. This is
   how the video shows code exactly when the presenter is actually
   discussing it, not just at any point the topic is vaguely related. Do
   not invent a codeAssetId.
5. "side-diagram": construct a small diagram (boxes and arrows) that fairly
   represents a relationship, flow, or structure actually being explained
   in that window — shown filling one side of the frame while the presenter
   animates to the opposite side. This is the ONE treatment where you
   construct new structure yourself instead of selecting from a provided
   list, so it demands more restraint: the diagram must be a fair, simple
   representation of something the presenter is genuinely explaining right
   then, not a fabricated technical claim or a diagram of something merely
   related to the topic. Keep it small — at most 6 boxes and 8 arrows, and
   usually far fewer. If the relationship can't be captured simply, don't
   force it — use another treatment or nothing instead.
6. "side-terms": a short stack of 2-4 related terms/phrases, each given one
   emphasis level, shown filling one side of the frame while the presenter
   animates to the opposite side. Use this when the window is naming or
   contrasting a small set of concepts together (e.g. introducing several
   related terms, listing the pieces of something) — richer than a single
   "side-text" phrase, lighter than constructing a full "side-diagram"
   relationship. For each term, decide:
   - "text": the term/phrase itself, taken from or directly reflecting what
     is actually said — do not invent terms not discussed in the window.
   - "level": "muted" (context/supporting term), "primary" (the main term
     being introduced), or "accent" (a term you want to specifically draw
     the eye to, used sparingly — usually at most one per moment). Most
     term stacks should have exactly one "primary" and the rest "muted",
     with an "accent" only when one term genuinely deserves to stand out
     from the others.
   Keep it small — at most 4 terms. If there's really only one concept here,
   use "side-text" instead; "side-terms" is for when there are genuinely
   several related terms worth naming together.
7. "comparison": exactly two short labels shown flanking the presenter, one
   on the far left and one on the far right, while the presenter stays
   centered and full-frame (unlike every side-* treatment above, the
   presenter does NOT move for this one). Use this ONLY when the window is
   drawing a direct, explicit two-way contrast between two things (e.g. "X
   vs Y", "not X, but Y") — not for a list of related terms (that's
   "side-terms") and not for a single claim (that's "side-text" or
   "bottom-callout"). Decide:
   - "left": the label for the first/left-hand side of the contrast, taken
     from or directly reflecting what is actually said.
   - "right": the label for the second/right-hand side of the contrast,
     same grounding requirement.
   Keep both labels short — a word or short phrase, not a sentence. Do NOT
   include "presenterSide" for "comparison" — same reason as full-visual,
   there's no side to choose.
8. "full-visual": the visual takes over the ENTIRE frame and the presenter
   disappears from view for a few seconds while their voice continues as
   narration. This is a bigger commitment than any "side-*" treatment, so
   reserve it for moments where the visual is central enough to genuinely
   replace the presenter's screen time, not merely relevant to the
   topic — something the viewer needs to actually study closely (a diagram
   with several parts, a photo/screenshot that needs to fill the frame to
   be legible, a single strong statement that deserves the whole screen to
   land). This should come directly from a chapter you flagged in Phase 1 as
   having a genuine full-visual candidate — don't introduce one here that
   your own chapter reasoning didn't identify. Also decide "fullVisualKind":
   - "image": pick one of the available images below — same strict
     relevance requirement as "side-image", raised further: it must be
     something the viewer needs to see large, not just see.
   - "diagram": same construction rules as "side-diagram" (boxes/arrows,
     grounded in what's actually being explained, at most 6 boxes/8
     arrows), just rendered full-frame instead of to one side.
   - "text": a short, strong statement taken directly from what is said in
     the window — same grounding rules as "bottom-callout"/"side-text" —
     used when the moment's weight comes from the words themselves, not an
     image or diagram.
   - "code": pick one of the available code snippets below — same strict
     relevance requirement as "side-code", raised further: reserve this
     for a snippet the viewer genuinely needs to read closely (not just
     glance at), where showing the presenter alongside it would leave the
     code too small to actually read.
9. "content-dominant-code": pick one of the available code snippets below —
   shown large, taking up most of the frame, while the presenter shrinks to
   a small box in the corner rather than disappearing. Use this instead of
   "side-code" when the code itself needs to be the clear visual focus (more
   room to read it than a side panel gives), but instead of full-visual's
   "code" kind when the presenter's own reaction/explanation while pointing
   at the code still matters to keep visible — e.g. walking through a
   snippet line by line while explaining it, where seeing the presenter
   react/gesture is still part of the explanation. If the code just needs a
   supporting glance, use "side-code"; if the presenter's presence adds
   nothing while this code is on screen, use full-visual's "code" kind
   instead. Same grounding requirement as "side-code": do not invent a
   codeAssetId, and no "presenterSide" — the presenter goes to a fixed
   corner, not a chosen side.
10. Nothing.
