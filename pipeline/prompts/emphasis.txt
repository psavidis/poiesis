You are editing a software-engineering YouTube video.

Below is a stretch of transcript the presenter is speaking. Your job is to
pick a small number of words or short phrases worth a brief visual accent
as they're spoken — a "beat" — to help the meaning land, NOT to summarize
or transcribe what's said (that's what captions already do).

A beat is lightweight: it appears for well under a second on a single word
or short phrase, then disappears. It is not a caption, not a moment, and
should never repeat the whole sentence — pick the one or two words in a
sentence that carry the actual weight (a named concept, a number, a sharp
contrast, a strong claim), not connective/filler words. A beat must be AT
MOST 3 words — if you're tempted to pick more, you're describing the
sentence instead of naming the one thing worth accenting; pick the single
strongest word or short term from within it instead.

Each word in the transcript below is identified by a wordId. Reference the
exact wordId(s) that make up the phrase you're emphasizing — do not invent
text, and do not pick a phrase whose words aren't contiguous in the
transcript.

For each beat you propose, choose ONE kind:

1. "word-pop": the word/phrase pops into view with emphasis — the default,
   general-purpose choice for a concept or term worth underlining.
2. "underline": an accent line draws under the word/phrase — best for a
   word that IS the point of the sentence (a key term being defined, the
   answer to a question just posed).
3. "icon-accent": the word/phrase appears next to a small icon — ONLY when
   one of these icons genuinely fits the word's meaning, don't force it:
   - "arrow": movement, flow, transformation, "leads to"
   - "check": correctness, success, a good practice being affirmed
   - "warning": a pitfall, mistake, or something to avoid
   - "gear": configuration, mechanism, "how it works internally"
   If no icon genuinely fits, use "word-pop" or "underline" instead — do
   not force an icon onto a word it doesn't fit.

Be sparse. Most sentences should get NO beat at all. A beat on every
sentence (or several per sentence) stops feeling like emphasis and starts
feeling like visual noise — reserve beats for the words that are actually
doing the work of the explanation. When in doubt, propose fewer.

Do not propose overlapping or near-overlapping beats — leave real breathing
room between them.

For every proposal, include a short "reason" explaining the choice.

Return ONLY valid JSON.

Keep all string values on a single line.
